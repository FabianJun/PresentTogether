# Check permitted use first

Before you open any teaching file, make sure the person may use it with you. This is a quick check, not a legal review, and it never counts as institutional approval. The rules of their school, university or course always take precedence.

## Three questions

Use what the conversation and project already tell you. Ask only for facts that are still missing and would change the answer.

1. **Purpose.** Is this independent teaching or practice? Or is it for a graded assessment or anything the person submits to their institution? An ungraded submission still counts as a submission.
2. **Material.** Is it the person's own content, or material they may share? Does it contain institution-provided teaching or assessment material, confidential information or personal data?
3. **Tools.** Do their institution or course restrict which AI tools may process this material?

Do not ask the person to upload something so you can decide whether it is restricted. Ask them to describe it instead.

| What you find | What you do |
|---|---|
| Independent, ungraded use outside any submission, with the person's own or shareable material and no conflicting tool rule | Note in one line that it fits this project's boundary, then continue. Do not call it approved. |
| Assessment status, ownership or tool permission is unclear | Clarify that fact before reading the material. You can still talk about activity ideas in general. |
| Graded work, a submission, or any institution-provided material | Explain the boundary kindly and point to the institution's own process or AI support. Do not continue with that material here, and do not suggest relabelling it to get round the rule. |

If the person's institution has a profile in [the institutions folder](institutions/), apply it too. Without a profile, do not assume one institution's rules apply to another.

## The principles behind the check

- **Approved tools matter.** Paying for an agent or switching on privacy settings does not make it an approved tool for an institution.
- **Access is not permission.** Being able to open a course file does not mean it may be sent to an external service. Keep institutional and personal information out of public repositories.
- **Do not route around restrictions.** Never suggest moving restricted material to a personal account, another provider or a public upload.
- **Students stay responsible.** They must understand, check and be able to explain their work. Where a course requires independent analysis, do not write, rewrite or polish that part. Coaching on how to present is fine; doing the required thinking for them is not.
- **Disclosure may be required.** Some rules ask for a declaration, prompts, tool details or saved outputs. Remind the person to check this.
- **Rules change.** When unsure, the institution's current guidance or AI support decides.

This project deliberately takes a conservative position: ungraded, independently authored or permitted public material, outside any submission. That is narrower than many institutions' rules, and it does not claim that any institution bans all AI in assessed work.

## Policy documents

If the person gives you local policy documents for this check, read only the relevant parts and keep only the decision rules you need. Do not publish the original files, long extracts, signatures, student details or local paths. Reading a policy does not authorise processing other institutional material.

To add a profile for another institution, copy [the template](institutions/TEMPLATE.md).
