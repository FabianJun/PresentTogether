# What this game models

The class advises two fictional AI labs, OpenBrain and DeepCent. Both want to stay ahead. Every participant chooses **Cooperate** or **Race** in each of four rounds: **2025, 2026, 2027, Transition**.

Cooperate keeps safety checks and oversight. Race puts speed first. There is no separate policy ballot and no later change to the competitive payoff matrix.

## Two different measures

**Competitive points** show each side's advantage in the game. **Human control** is a shared teaching score. It does not measure the probability of a catastrophe, and 65 is not a scientifically established safety boundary.

| Your side / other side | Cooperate | Race |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Cooperate** | 3 / 3 | 0 / 5 |
| **Race** | 5 / 0 | 1 / 1 |

The first number is your side's points for that round. The second is the other side's. Totals add across rounds. Under these competitive rules, Race gives a side more points for either fixed choice by its rival. Yet both sides earn more from mutual cooperation than mutual racing. That is the prisoner's dilemma illustrated here.

Human control starts at **50**. Mutual cooperation adds **15**. Each side that races removes **10**: one racing side means −10; both racing means −20. The score stays between 0 and 100. The teaching target is **65**.

| Four-round path | Final points, A / B | Final control |
|---|---:|---:|
| Both cooperate every round | 12 / 12 | 100, capped |
| A races alone in round 1; both cooperate afterwards | 14 / 9 | 85 |
| Both race in round 1; both cooperate afterwards | 10 / 10 | 75 |
| Both race in every round | 4 / 4 | 0, floored |

Order matters near a floor. Three mutual races followed by mutual cooperation end at 15 because the third race hits zero. Cooperation followed by three mutual races ends at 5. Use the actual round history rather than an uncapped shortcut.

The capability line is a separate illustrative trajectory. It starts at 20. In the first three rounds, mutual cooperation adds 12, 16 and 20; if either side races, the additions are 20, 26 and 32. Transition adds 40. Capability is capped at 100. These are authored model values, not forecasts from AI 2027.

## Roles and counting

Participants join sides alternately. Each side has one government representative, lab leader, safety researcher and public representative. Additional participants are council members weighing growth against safety. Every participant votes; roles do not give extra votes.

The majority of submitted C/R votes decides the side's choice. A tied vote follows that side's government representative, if they submitted a vote. Otherwise a tie means Race. A single submitted vote is decisive. Missing votes are not secretly counted as Race. If a side has no votes at all, a moderator may supply a labelled decision so the session can continue.

The game reveals both sides together. Students cannot see the other side's current choice beforehand. Results show the vote split, any tie-break or moderator decision, round points and cumulative totals.

## What the debrief should establish

Ask who finished ahead, whether oversight kept pace, and who carries the risks or costs. Cooperation involves slower progress and reliance on the other side. Racing can give an advantage while exposing workers, users and people outside both sides to risks they did not choose.

The presentation supports cooperation backed by workable checks, credible enforcement, technical safeguards and human oversight. Those institutions are discussed as possible ways to change incentives; the playable game keeps the original matrix throughout.

AI 2027 provides the scenario inspiration. It is a speculative scenario, not a deadline or established prediction. This classroom model omits many real-world dynamics, including unequal power, uncertain information and the practical difficulty of enforcing agreements. Its outcome is evidence about the class's choices **under these rules**, not a forecast of geopolitical behaviour. Sources and distinctions appear in the slides and speaker notes.
